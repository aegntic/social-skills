## Design Context

### Users
Content creators who post across multiple social platforms. They want speed and control — compose once, publish everywhere, and see what the app has learned about their voice over time. Their context is fast-paced: drafting between meetings, scheduling a week of content in one sitting, checking engagement on the go. The interface should feel like a creative tool they enjoy opening, not a chore to navigate.

### Brand Personality
**Bold. Playful. Energetic.** Social Skills has opinions and isn't afraid to show them. It treats cross-posting as a creative act, not a logistics problem. The memory layer is the soul — the app *remembers* you, and that should feel alive and personal, not like a database.

### Aesthetic Direction
**Light, airy, editorial — with bold, expressive energy.** Think a well-designed magazine that happens to be a working tool. Framer and Arc Browser are the north stars for distinctiveness and confidence, but executed on a light canvas rather than dark. Strong typography carries the personality; layout asymmetry creates rhythm; color is confident but never gradient-washed.

**References**: Framer (expressive confidence, bold type), Arc Browser (distinctive, opinionated, joyful), editorial print design (magazine grids, generous whitespace, hierarchy through scale).

**Anti-references**: Hypey crypto aesthetics (gradient text, glassmorphism, neon-on-dark). Generic SaaS dashboards (grey, corporate, forgettable). Overly playful/toy-like UIs (too many colors, rounded everything, no sophistication).

### Design Principles

1. **Typography is the hero.** Large, confident display type paired with a refined body font. Hierarchy through dramatic scale shifts, not subtle weight changes. The page should feel composed even before a single color is applied.

2. **Editorial composition, not card grid.** Break the grid intentionally. Use asymmetry, varied column widths, and generous whitespace the way a print magazine does. Not everything lives in a rounded container.

3. **Confident color, not decorative color.** Bold accent colors used purposefully — to highlight, to separate, to energize. Never gradient text, never glassmorphism, never color for color's sake. Tint neutrals toward the brand hue for subconscious cohesion.

4. **Motion conveys life, not decoration.** The memory layer should feel alive — staggered reveals, smooth state transitions, elements that settle into place with natural deceleration. Motion is the "energetic" in bold/playful/energetic.

5. **Progressive disclosure over clutter.** Start simple and confident. Realsophistication through interaction, not by showing everything at once. The compose surface should feel like opening a fresh notebook, not a control panel.
