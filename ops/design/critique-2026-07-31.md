# Critique — socialskills.ninja

Method: live HTML pull of home (75KB), compose (12KB / 404), journey (24KB), plus `.impeccable.md` design context. Critique is diagnostic — no code changes here.

## What's working

- **Type scale is doing real work.** `text-4xl sm:text-6xl md:text-7xl font-black tracking-tight leading-[1.05]` on the H1, paired with `text-4xl sm:text-6xl font-black` on section H2s. That's a 1.5×+ ratio between the hero and body sizes — hierarchy reads at a glance.
- **Clay primitives are visibly deployed.** `.clay`/`.clay-pressable` on the nav button, rounded-clay-sm, clay-ink. The visual language is consistent in the markup even if the surface area is uneven.
- **Two font families, paired.** `a65324f0...` and `ee40bb09...` preloaded as woff2 — one display, one body. The pairing is the right instinct; whether the *choice* is right needs visual review.
- **Editorial section breaks.** 9 sections, `border-t border-slate-300/60` between each, generous `py-20`/`py-24`. The rhythm is there.

## What's wrong — by priority

### 1. Compose route 404s — kill the nav link or ship the route

`/compose` returns a 404 (12KB body, h1="404"). It's linked in the main nav. Every visitor who clicks "Compose" lands on a dead page. This is the single most credibility-destroying thing on the site right now, ahead of any aesthetic issue.

**Options:** ship the route on Vercel as a serverless function, or hide the nav link until it's real. The lazy fix is the second (one-line removal). The right fix is shipping the route.

### 2. The site has no visible identity in screenshots

The H1 color is `text-white`. The hero is presumably on a dark or saturated background, but the section after the hero (`#features`) is on a light clay surface. There's no visible brand mark, no logo, no mark — `<head>` returns no `<svg>` or `<img>` for branding. The page can be mistaken for any Tailwind starter.

**Fix:** introduce a visible logo/wordmark, ideally as a custom-drawn SVG with character (not a font-as-logo). Pair it with a hue-tinted neutral that grounds the brand in the page.

### 3. Every section heading is `font-black` at the same scale

`text-4xl sm:text-6xl font-black` is used on **8 of 9 section H2s**. There's no variation in weight or scale between sections. The magazine-rhythm principle in your design context ("Hierarchy through dramatic scale shifts") is being undermined by repetition.

**Fix:** alternate scale per section — make the most important section's H2 actually larger (e.g. `text-5xl sm:text-7xl`), let quieter sections drop to `text-3xl sm:text-5xl font-bold` (not `font-black`). Use weight to differentiate quiet vs. loud.

### 4. Borderline AI-slop patterns present

Pulled from the live markup:

- **`border-t border-slate-300/60` on every section** — 9 instances. Sub-1px hairline dividers between sections are fine, but using exactly the same divider everywhere flattens rhythm. Break it: full-bleed color shift on one section, no divider on another, oversized type as divider on a third.
- **Multiple `py-20` and `py-24`** — uniform vertical rhythm. Vary: `py-12` after a section that already has visual weight, `py-32` before the most important one.
- **Centered H2s alternating with left-aligned H2s** without a clear reason. The center alignment feels like a default, not a decision.

### 5. The journey page is the most distinctive surface — and it's not surfaced

`/journey` has the kind of content that *earns* the editorial framing: "Learning timeline", "Clusters", "Recent signals". This is the memory-layer story the design context calls the "soul" of the app. It's buried two clicks deep. The home page should let it breathe — even one signal block on the home page would do more for the brand than another feature card.

### 6. The font choice needs visual review

`a65324f0...` and `ee40bb09...` are preloaded. The skill spec explicitly bans the reflex list (Fraunces, Inter, Space Grotesk, IBM Plex, etc.). Without seeing the rendered font I can't confirm — but the `a65324f0...` filename pattern (Vercel/Next.js font hashing) is consistent with the popular reflex fonts, including Space Grotesk, which appears in many "bold startup" sites.

**Action:** screenshot the rendered H1 in a browser, compare against the skill's reflex list. If it matches, swap for something less common — Pangram Pangram, Klim, Velvetyne, or a Future Fonts face.

### 7. The site has no motion choreography visible in the markup

The design context calls motion the "energetic in bold/playful/energetic". The HTML is static — no animation classes, no `data-aos`, no Framer Motion imports, no transition utilities. This isn't a bug, but it's an unfulfilled promise. A staggered reveal on the hero (H1 → subtitle → CTA, ~50ms apart) and a soft ease-out-quart on section scroll would land hard.

### 8. The primary CTA copy is generic

`Publish to 5` is one of the few legible button labels. It tells the user what the system does, not what they get. Compare:
- Current: "Publish to 5"
- More confident: "Ship to all five"
- Or with memory-layer flavor: "Post it everywhere, learn from the result"

Your design context calls for "opinions and isn't afraid to show them" — the CTAs should have voice.

## What's missing

- **No visible logo/mark.** See #2.
- **No favicon-check.** Worth a separate pass.
- **No "what is this?" moment for a first-time visitor.** A new user lands on a wall of feature sections. There's no single image, quote, or signature interaction that says "this is Social Skills, here's what makes it different." Editorial design lives or dies on its opening spread.
- **No visible state of the memory layer on the home page.** The product's differentiator is invisible from the marketing surface.

## The single most leveraged fix

If you ship exactly one thing: make the home page open with a signature moment that uses the journey/memory layer visually — a "this is what the app knows about you" panel, a recent-signal strip, or a single quote from a creator with the cluster it lives in. That single change does more for the brand than any combination of the polish items above, because it makes the product's differentiator the first thing a visitor encounters.

## Recommended next skills (in order)

1. `impeccable polish` — once the home page has its signature moment.
2. `impeccable bolder` — if the result still feels too quiet.
3. `impeccable animate` — to add the motion choreography the design context calls for.
4. `impeccable typeset` — if the font check (#6) lands on a swap.

Skip: `adapt`, `distill`, `quieter` — the page is already quiet; it needs more voice, not less.
